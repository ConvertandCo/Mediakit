## Reel 20 — The surprise at checkout (Fri 13 Nov) — v2 script (11 Nov)
VO
[curious] Eight ninety-nine. She's ready to pay. … Shipping, forty-nine. … Platform fee, nineteen. … Nine sixty-seven. [serious] She's gone. In Baymard's research, forty percent of shoppers who abandoned checkout said extra costs were too high. Twelve percent couldn't even see the total up front. [warmly] She wasn't upset about forty-nine rupees. She was upset she found out last. So show delivery on the product page. Put "free delivery above nine ninety-nine" right under the price. [serious] Let the last number… be the first number she saw. Save this.
- Example prices (₹899 + ₹49 + ₹19 = ₹967) are illustrative; label "example" on screen.
- Fact: Baymard (updated 28 Oct 2026), US shoppers excluding "just browsing": 40% extra costs too high; 12% couldn't see total up front.

## Reel 21 — Will it arrive before the wedding? (Mon 16 Nov)
VO
[curious] Forty-six lakh weddings. Forty-five days. [serious] That's CAIT's count for last year's wedding season. Every one of them needs a gift that arrives on time. [warmly] So don't make them guess. Show "get it by" with a real date on the product page. Add gift wrap and a gift message. Post your last order date for each wedding week. [serious] A gift that arrives late… is a gift they won't buy from you. Save this.

Alt hook to test: "Here's what a wedding guest checks before buying your product."
Film techniques: ticking clock (a calendar with a wedding date circled, days tearing off); dramatic irony (the viewer sees the parcel still in the warehouse while the guest is dressing); bookend (opens on an empty gift table, ends with the parcel on it).
Visual idea (storybook): a wedding mandap at night with a gift table; a little courier racing a moon-clock; the product page gains a "Get it by Sat, 21 Nov" line in orange.
Facts
- Fact: CAIT estimate for the 2025 season (1 Nov – 14 Dec 2025): 46 lakh weddings, ₹6.5 lakh crore spend (afaqs, 21 Nov 2025). No 2026 estimate found yet; the script says "last year".
- Opinion: "get it by" date, gift wrap and cut-off dates are our recommendations.

## Reel 22 — The customer who asks AI first (Thu 19 Nov)
VO
[curious] Your next customer might ask an AI before they ever see your store. [serious] Shopify says orders from AI search grew three point three times in a year. The volume is still small… but it's growing. [warmly] And AI can't read your banner. It reads your product data. So write what it is, who it's for, the size, the ingredients and the price… in text, not inside images. Add real FAQs. [serious] If the AI can't read it, it can't recommend it. Save this.

Alt hook to test: "Here's what an AI sees when it looks at your product page."
Film techniques: POV shot (we see the page through the AI's "eyes": images are grey blanks, only text lights up); reversal (the beautiful banner is invisible to it); rack focus from banner to plain text specs.
Visual idea (collage): a paper robot with a magnifying glass reads a pinned product page; image areas turn to blank grey boxes; the text lines glow yellow. Fix: specs and FAQs written as text cards.
Facts
- Fact: Shopify Q3 2026 results (3 Nov 2026): orders from AI searches up 3.3x year over year; AI traffic to storefronts up 2.6x; Shopify Catalog-powered searches convert at about 2x general AI search. Finkelstein: "the volume is still small". Shopify did not disclose the base (ppc.land).
- Interpretation: "AI reads product data, not banners" follows from how catalog-based AI shopping works; "can't recommend it" is our framing, not a measured claim.

## Reel 23 — You can't beat 10 minutes (Mon 23 Nov) — v2 script (11 Nov)
VO
[curious] A quick-commerce app can get there in ten minutes. You can't. … Relax. [serious] In a LocalCircles survey of festive quick-commerce shoppers, only twenty-two percent named speedy delivery as a priority. Seventy-one percent named the best price or value. Sixty-five percent named quality. [warmly] So stop racing a rocket on a scooter. Show what makes your product worth the price. Show the full total, with no surprise charges. Give a delivery date… and keep it. [serious] They're not counting minutes. They're counting value. Save this.
- Fact: LocalCircles via Business Today (27 Oct 2026): value 71%, quality 65%, low/no extra charges 43%, speedy delivery 22%. Sample size not stated.
- Don't name a quick-commerce brand on screen.

## Reel 24 — Your ad is bored (Thu 26 Nov)
VO
[curious] Your best ad isn't broken. Your audience is just bored of it. [serious] In a survey of over a hundred Indian D2C founders, sixty-two percent reported creative fatigue. The same ads stopped holding ROAS, even as spend went up. [warmly] So don't remake the same video in five colours. Make a new angle: a problem, an objection, a comparison, an unboxing. Test one each week. Keep the winner. [serious] Feed your ad something new… before you feed it more money. Save this.

Alt CTA to test (caption): "Send this to a founder who's about to raise their ad budget." (tests sends vs comments)
Film techniques: rule of three (the same ad plays three times; the viewer yawns more each time; the fourth is a new angle and the eyes open); montage for the four angles; callback to the yawning viewer at the end.
Visual idea (collage): a paper TV showing the same ad on loop with a counter "seen 7×"; a viewer cut-out slowly slumps. Four new ad cards fan out; three crumple, one glows.
Facts
- Fact: DSG Consumer Partners report (via Entrepreneur India / Third Eyesight, 15 Dec 2025), 100+ Indian D2C founders and operators: 62% report creative fatigue (repeated ads stop sustaining ROAS even as spend rises); 70%+ rely on Meta as their primary acquisition channel.
- Opinion: one new angle per week is our recommendation, not a finding.

## Reel 16 — Fifty milliseconds (Mon 30 Nov)
VO
[curious] You have fifty milliseconds. [serious] In a two thousand six study, people judged how a website looked in the first fifty milliseconds… and that snap verdict matched what they decided after looking longer. [warmly] That's less than a blink. So before anyone reads a word, they've already judged your first screen. One clear photo. One clear line. Nothing fighting for attention. [serious] Make the first glance count. Save this.

Alt hook to test: "Here's how long a shopper takes to judge your store."
Film techniques: a camera-shutter freeze frame at 0.05 s; Kuleshov-style pairing (the same shopper face next to a cluttered page vs a clean one reads as annoyed vs relaxed); silence before "fifty".
Visual idea (storybook): a giant camera shutter clicks once; two first screens flash for a split second each; the cluttered one shatters, the clean one stays.
Facts
- Fact: Lindgaard et al., Carleton University, Behaviour & Information Technology (2006): visual-appeal judgments formed within 50 ms and matched longer viewing (Nature, Jan 2006).
- Interpretation: the study measured visual appeal, not trust or buying.
